For the final deliverable of this project, I plan to run an A/B test designed to evaluate the role of strategically-placed statistics in digital consumer decision-making. The website is an informational guide to small businesses in Iowa City, intended for local residents or visitors to the area. The first of its five pages is the Home page, where users will find the primary Call-to-Action, a prominent, high-contrast button reading “Explore the Stores Now”. When clicked, this button internally links to a page entitled “Local Shops Directory,” which contains a categorized list of the many businesses featured in this guide. The A version given to the AI model will be the site as it is, with the button under the page heading and a two-sentence overview. The B version will have added text copy under the button, highlighting a statistic intended to persuade site users to shop from the local businesses. The added text box reads: “A study conducted by American Express, the corporation that founded Small Business Saturday in 2010, found that for every dollar spent at a small business in America, 68¢ remains in the local community.” Everything else on the site will remain the same between both versions. My hypothesis is that when the AI model is given Version B, with the statistic, the AI will output the decision to click the button for a higher proportion of the trials than for the Version A design with just the button. I believe that moving the relevant evidence to top-of-mind for users will consciously or subconsciously position clicking as the “right” thing to do, making visitors more likely to. I will use the Sonnet 5.5 model from the Claude.ai website on the High effort setting. The prompt given to the model is as follows: “You are a young professional in your 20’s who recently moved to Iowa City, and you are looking for somewhere to shop for a gift for a friend. Read the home page of this site and decide whether or not you would click on the “Explore the Stores Now” button that shows when you open the page. Answer with either yes or no followed by one sentence explaining why. \[page link to A or B\].” I will use the IF and RANDBETWEEN functions in Excel to select either A or B 100 times, randomly selecting which trials will receive each version. The outcome is the decision to click, and it is being measured with yes or no, which will be represented by 0 and 1 for quantitative analysis. Only a yes will be considered a 1 and anything else will be a 0\. Every trial will be measured and counted, and trials will continue until and end at exactly one hundred, expected to be reached 11/16/2026. Analysis will focus on comparing the proportion of yes answers for Version A and for Version B, and the distribution and p-value, with an alpha of 0.05, will determine whether findings are statistically significant. A significantly higher proportion of yes answers for Version B would support the hypothesis.
